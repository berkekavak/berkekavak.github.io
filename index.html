Approach

Prototype early. Ship on your stack. Hand it over.

A working prototype is worth a thousand slides. I get to one fast, then make it boring enough to run in production.

01 · Discover

Find the metric

One to two weeks on your data and your process. We agree on the use case, the success metric and what "done" looks like.

02 · Prototype

Prove it on real data

An interactive prototype your team can click through, built on your actual data. Decide to continue with evidence, not a deck.

03 · Productionize

Make it reliable

Deployment on your cloud with monitoring, evaluation and retraining in place. Integrated into the tools people already use.

04 · Hand over

Your team owns it

Documentation, training and a clean handover. The goal is a system you run without me, and a reason to call again.

Business metric first

Model accuracy is a means. Retention, cost, throughput or error rate is what we report on.

Your stack, your cloud

AWS, Databricks, GCP or Azure. No new vendor lock-in, and nothing that leaves your environment.

No black boxes

Readable code, documented decisions and evaluation you can rerun. You should be able to audit everything.

About

Applied research, judged on delivery.

I'm Berke, a Berlin-based data scientist and AI consultant. I work at the intersection of applied research and delivery: comfortable reading the paper, but judged on whether the system runs in production and moves a business metric.

Since 2023 I have run the AI and GenAI portfolio at BeeBI Consulting for EU and US customers, from presales through post-deployment across five concurrent clients. Before that I was an early team member at TAZI AI through 4× growth and a $4.6M round, packaging its AI solutions into repeatable offerings and shipping retention, NLP and clinical models for US and European clients.

My master's research at Boğaziçi University on biomedical entity normalization with LLMs was published at the BioCreative VIII SympTEMIST track. An industrial engineering and economics double major at Koç University taught me to treat ML problems as business problems first.

I work in English and Turkish, and speak good German.
